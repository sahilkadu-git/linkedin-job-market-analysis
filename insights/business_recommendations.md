# Business Recommendations — LinkedIn Job Market Analysis
**Analyst:** Sahil Kadu  
**Dataset:** LinkedIn Job Postings 2023–2024 (5,000 postings, 33 domains)  
**Tools:** Python · MySQL · Excel · Power BI  

---

## Executive Summary

Analysis of 5,000 LinkedIn job postings across 33 job domains revealed that **domain choice is the single biggest salary driver** — outweighing experience level, city, and work type combined. Legal roles pay 7.2x more than Manufacturing in the same job market. Supply Chain roles attract the most competition yet rank 26th in salary. Full-time roles offer the same pay as Contract with 3.7x fewer applicants.

---

## Finding 1 — Domain Choice Determines Salary More Than Experience

**Data:** Legal avg salary = $100,487 | Manufacturing avg salary = $14,002 | Gap = 618%

Entry-level salary in Legal ($60,000+) exceeds Director-level salary in Manufacturing ($35,000). This means a fresher who targets the right domain will out-earn a 10-year veteran in the wrong one.

**Recommendation:**  
Job seekers should prioritise domain targeting over experience accumulation. The top 5 domains by salary are:

| Rank | Domain | Avg Salary |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | Legal | $100,487 |
| 2 | Accounting/Auditing | $47,789 |
| 3 | Strategy/Planning | $45,912 |
| 4 | Marketing | $45,680 |
| 5 | Finance | $41,916 |

**Action:** If currently in a low-paying domain, upskill toward Legal, Finance or Accounting functions. Even a lateral move into a higher-paying domain at the same experience level yields a 40–60% salary increase.

---

## Finding 2 — Supply Chain Has the Worst Risk-Reward Ratio

**Data:** Supply Chain avg applicants = 43.8 per posting (highest in dataset) | avg salary = $21,457 (rank 26 of 33)

Supply Chain roles attract nearly 6x more applicants than Legal roles (43.8 vs 4.2) yet pay less than half the salary. This is the worst opportunity score in the entire dataset at 738 vs Legal's 19,331.

**Recommendation:**  
Avoid Supply Chain as a primary career target. If already in Supply Chain, pivot toward adjacent domains with better pay — specifically Consulting, Strategy/Planning or Business Development which share similar skill sets but pay 30–120% more.

---

## Finding 3 — Full-time Roles Are Significantly Less Competitive Than Contract

**Data:** Full-time avg applicants = 2.4 | Contract avg applicants = 8.9 | Salary difference = $178 only

Contract roles attract 3.7x more applicants for virtually the same salary ($29,955 vs $30,133). Candidates are competing harder for the same financial outcome.

**Recommendation:**  
Entry-level and early-career job seekers should exclusively target full-time roles. The marginal salary benefit of contract work ($178/year) does not justify the 3.7x increase in competition. Filtering job searches to Full-time only immediately improves application success probability.

---

## Finding 4 — The Entry to Associate Jump Is the Most Valuable Career Move

**Data:** Entry level avg = $15,788 | Associate avg = $37,638 | Increase = +138%

The single largest salary jump in the career ladder is from Entry Level to Associate — a 138% increase. By comparison, Associate to Mid-Senior is actually a decrease in this dataset ($37,638 → $30,045) due to data composition. Director level reaches $68,759 — 4.4x entry level.

**Recommendation:**  
Early-career professionals should aggressively pursue Associate-level roles rather than staying at Entry level for extended periods. Even 1–2 years of targeted experience is enough to justify the Associate title jump. Staying at Entry level beyond 18 months is a salary opportunity cost of $21,850/year.

---

## Finding 5 — New York and Seattle Offer the Best Location Value

**Data:** Seattle avg salary = $76,697 | New York avg = $75,857 | Houston avg = $20,733

Houston has the 2nd most job postings in the dataset (77 postings) but pays only $20,733 average — the worst salary-to-volume ratio of any major city. New York has the most postings (107) AND the 2nd highest salary. Seattle has fewer postings (30) but the highest salary.

**Recommendation:**  
For remote-capable candidates, target companies headquartered in Seattle or New York even when working remotely — salaries are often location-anchored to HQ. For on-site candidates, New York offers the best combination of volume and salary. Avoid Houston-based roles unless the specific company offers above-market compensation.

---

## Finding 6 — Sweet Spot Domains Combine High Salary With Low Competition

**Data:** Sweet Spot domains (salary > $40,000 AND applicants < 8): Health Care Provider, Legal, General Business, Accounting/Auditing, Finance, Science, Production, Human Resources, Business Development

These 9 domains represent the best opportunity in the market — above-average salary combined with below-average competition.

**Recommendation:**  
Job seekers should concentrate applications on Sweet Spot domains. With only 2.5–8 applicants per posting and salaries above $40,000, these domains offer the highest probability of success per application sent.

**Priority order for entry-level candidates:**
1. Accounting/Auditing — 64 postings, $55,178 avg, 4.1 applicants
2. Finance — 81 postings, $49,105 avg, 4.3 applicants  
3. Human Resources — 46 postings, $49,164 avg, 8.0 applicants
4. Health Care Provider — 33 postings, $54,403 avg, 2.5 applicants

---

## Overall Recommendation — The 3-Step Job Search Strategy

Based on all 8 queries, the optimal job search strategy for a data professional is:

**Step 1 — Target the right domain**  
Focus exclusively on Sweet Spot domains: Legal, Finance, Accounting, Health Care. Avoid Supply Chain, Manufacturing and Education which have poor salary-to-competition ratios.

**Step 2 — Filter to Full-time only**  
Remove Contract, Part-time and Temporary from job search filters. Full-time roles have 3.7x fewer applicants for the same salary.

**Step 3 — Prioritise New York and Seattle**  
These two cities offer the best salary benchmarks. Even for hybrid roles, companies in these cities tend to pay above-market rates across all experience levels.

Following this 3-step strategy based on the data improves expected salary outcome by an estimated **45–60%** compared to an unfiltered job search approach.
